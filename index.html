Your tracking code works. The problem was what Google Ads counted as a conversion: one lead often counted twice, and forms that are not leads counted too. Google bids toward whatever it counts, so the count has to be right.

1One lead counted twice

A lead counted once when the form went through, and again when HubSpot marked it as an MQL. Both counted as a full conversion.

Example: PEO Recruitment, Existing Markets counted 21 form fills and 21 MQLs, so 42 conversions from about 21 people.

The fixGoogle Ads counts one conversion for each lead: the form fill. MQLs move to reporting only.

2Every HubSpot form on the site counted as a lead

The lead tracking fired for any HubSpot form, so a media inquiry, a broker event sign-up or a "CoAd Qualified" badge request counted as a lead in Google Ads, Microsoft Ads and GA4.

The fixThe tracking will check which form was sent and skip those three. Every other form still counts, including any new form you add later.

✓What counts after the change

Google Ads learns from one thing: a real lead form sent. MQLs, SQLs and Opportunities stay in your reports by campaign, so we can see which campaigns bring leads your sales team wants. SQLs are still too few for Google to learn from (7 in the last 31 days). When there are enough, we can talk about bidding on them.

The reports already show something to look at: the two payroll campaigns spent $40,114 (27% of the account) for 81 form fills and about 3 MQLs. We'll go over that with you on Friday.

What you'll see: the Conversions column drops

Your leads don't change. Only the double count goes away. With the new setup, the same 31 days would have shown about 248 conversions in place of 388, one for each form fill.

The forms that are not leads come out too, so the real number will be a little lower.
